I'm writing this post with a big delay. On the past 11th of February 2026 I summited Toubkal. I wanted to write a bit on the challenge, the logistics and the story, as it is a very particular mountain, and although FFCAM has influence, the mountain is definetly different from the general alpine and mountaineering experience we can find in europe.

Following last years Alps trip, I was set on preparing myself for higher flies next year. I had the intent to do several winter-time smaller peaks in Spain (perfect, for me, to train to higher altitude situation - they can be very technical themselves) and have a solo alpine adventure that would require more concentration and logistics.

To my luck, the winter of 2026 was very rigorous. We had [record levels of precipitation](https://climate.copernicus.eu/exceptional-rainfall-and-flooding-parts-europe-and-north-africa-february) and very unstable weather. Very fun for alpine runs (Gredos had amazing conditions) and perfect to have a bit more of fun that we normally can't have, for example, ice climbing in Portugal was viable for the first time in 5 years!

I had a long-time idea to solo Monte Perdido: not too technical, no glaciar dangers and juicy enough so I could set my head for and higher flights. I got cold-feet: The weather looked weird for the time-window, the snow was unstable and I was reticent about having to take some snow-shoes, based on some stories I heard from similar conditions. I wasn't confident enough on a solo trip with these conditions. 

I checked my options and I remembered some conversations I had with my friend Charlie, where he told me about his Toubkal experience. I decided that a inter-continent solo trip, with increased logistics, although in a very much technically simplier mountain, seemed like a good enough challenge; specially as I decided to spice it up a bit: it would be done truly solo, unguided.

## Preparations

The route to Toubkal in winter is normally done in two dauys: first day you leave from Imlil at late morning, going up to the refuge at ~3000m passing through the stores and villages around, sleeping there and leaving at 6AM next day to summit. Normally you spend the night and either leave the next day or spend one or two more days trekking around. For all activities you should be accompaned by a local guide. This, obviously, is a big hurdle to soloing Toubkal.

I looked around for info and to understand the reason and exacly for what sections the guide is needed. Unfortunately, going to Toubkal is a cultural adventure; the main reason for the legal demand for a guide was the homicide of (Louisa and Maren)[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murders_of_Louisa_Vesterager_Jespersen_and_Maren_UelandA] a few years ago. There were some mountain-related accidents (for example, (crevasse falls)[https://www.express.co.uk/news/world/1691134/British-climber-dead-Morocco-Mount-Toubkal]), but the main reason unfortunately is the culture shock between western tourists and the partly-muslim population. Rashly or not, I decided to still go solo, without contacting an agency.
